PEB or conventional construction: how to choose

By Mahendra Heda, Executive Director · Published 2026-11-11

In short

A PEB (pre-engineered building) is a steel structure designed and fabricated as a system in a factory and assembled on site. Conventional construction casts reinforced concrete (RCC) on site. PEB usually suits wide, open, single-storey spaces that must be ready quickly. RCC usually suits multi-storey buildings and spaces with many partitions.

  • PEB is fabricated off site and assembled on site, which shortens site time.
  • PEB gives wide column-free spans. RCC suits multi-storey buildings.
  • The right choice depends on span, height, use, schedule and budget.

What each method is

A pre-engineered building (PEB) is a steel frame whose members are designed for a specific building, fabricated in a factory and bolted together on site. Roof and wall sheeting is fixed to the frame.

Conventional construction usually means a reinforced concrete (RCC) frame with masonry walls. Formwork and reinforcement are built on site and concrete is cast in place, so the building grows in stages as the concrete cures.

Where PEB fits

  • Warehouses, factories, workshops and sheds that need large open floor areas
  • Buildings where a short on-site period matters
  • Structures that may need to be extended later by adding bays

Where conventional construction fits

  • Multi-storey buildings such as offices, residential blocks, hospitals and colleges
  • Buildings with many walls, rooms and fixed partitions
  • Structures that need the thermal mass and fire resistance of concrete and masonry

Questions to settle before choosing

  1. How wide an open span does the use need?
  2. How many storeys, and what loads will the floors carry?
  3. When must the building be ready for use?
  4. Will the building need to grow later?
  5. What are the site conditions, and how far is the fabrication unit?

Foundations are still civil work

A PEB frame still stands on concrete foundations. Their design depends on the soil and the loads, and they must be accurate because the steel is made to fit them. Good civil work at the base is what lets a fast steel erection go smoothly.

MCC Group builds both PEB structures and conventional buildings, so the choice can be made on the merits of the project.

Common questions

What does PEB stand for?

PEB stands for pre-engineered building: a steel structure designed and fabricated as a system, then assembled on site.

Is PEB faster than RCC construction?

Usually the on-site period is shorter, because the steel is made in a factory while the foundations are prepared. The actual saving depends on the project.

Can a PEB building be extended?

Often yes, by adding bays, if this was allowed for in the original design.

Can PEB be used for multi-storey buildings?

It is mainly used for wide, low-rise buildings. Multi-storey buildings more commonly use RCC or other framed systems.
