Why timeline control decides project cost

By Mahendra Heda, Executive Director · Published 2026-11-11

In short

In infrastructure, time is money. Manpower, machinery, supervision and site overheads keep costing every day a project runs. When work is planned so that people and machines do not wait for each other, the project finishes sooner and costs stay under control.

  • Labour, machines and site overheads cost money on every day of the schedule.
  • Waiting is the hidden cost: a machine without material, or a crew without a machine.
  • Synchronising manpower and machinery is how time and cost are controlled together.

Time has a daily price

A civil project carries costs that continue whether or not work moves: site staff, supervision, machinery on hire or in ownership, and site overheads. Each extra day adds to them.

The cost of waiting

Most delays are not dramatic. They are small waits: concrete ready but no crew, a crew ready but no material, an excavator idle because the tippers are late. These waits add up.

Synchronising manpower and machinery

Planning means matching the number of people and machines to the work in each stage, so that each resource is used when it is needed. Manpower costs are then controlled, and the schedule holds.

  • Plan the work in stages with clear start and finish dates
  • Match crews, machines and materials to each stage
  • Order materials ahead of the stage that needs them
  • Review progress against the plan often, and correct early

Why this matters to the client

A project that finishes on time costs the client less in overruns and delivers the asset when it was needed. For the contractor, it keeps the project viable. This is why MCC Group puts quality, timeline and cost together.

Common questions

Why do delays increase cost?

Site staff, machinery and overheads continue to cost money for every extra day of the schedule.

What is synchronising manpower and machinery?

Planning people and machines so that each is used when needed, with no waiting.

Does finishing faster mean lower quality?

It should not. Good planning shortens waiting time, not the time that work needs to be done properly.
