Canal lining explained

Water · By Mahendra Heda, Executive Director · 2026-11-11 · 3 min read

In short

Canal lining is a layer, usually of concrete, placed on the bed and sides of a canal. It reduces water lost by seepage, protects the canal from erosion and helps water flow smoothly. The quality of the earthwork underneath decides how well the lining performs.

  • Lining reduces seepage, so more of the water reaches where it is needed.
  • The earthwork beneath the lining must be shaped and compacted to the design.
  • Joints and curing are where lining quality is won or lost.

What canal lining is

A canal carries water from a source to the fields or towns that use it. An unlined canal is cut into the ground, and some water soaks through the bed and sides. Lining places a layer over those surfaces to hold the water in the canal.

Why canals are lined

  • To reduce water lost to seepage
  • To protect the bed and banks from erosion
  • To give a smooth surface so water flows more easily
  • To make the canal section stable and easier to maintain

How the work is done

  1. Excavate and shape the canal to the designed section
  2. Compact the bed and sides to a firm, even base
  3. Fix any reinforcement and formwork or guides the design requires
  4. Place the concrete, using a paving machine where the section allows
  5. Form joints at the specified spacing
  6. Cure the concrete so it gains strength

What makes a lining last

  • A well-compacted base, so the lining is not left unsupported
  • Concrete placed to the specified thickness and finish
  • Properly formed and sealed joints
  • Enough curing time, with the surface kept moist

Earthwork first

Much of canal work is earthwork before it is concrete. Excavators, tippers and compaction equipment shape the section, and the lining follows. Canal and canal lining works are part of what MCC Group builds.

Common questions

What is canal lining?

A layer, usually of concrete, placed on the bed and sides of a canal.

Why are canals lined?

To reduce seepage loss, protect against erosion and let water flow smoothly.

What material is used for lining?

Concrete is widely used. The design decides the material and thickness.

Why does the base matter?

A lining placed over a soft or uneven base can crack. The ground beneath it must be compacted to the design.
