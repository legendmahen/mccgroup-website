The anatomy of a road: what lies beneath the surface

Roads · By Mahendra Heda, Executive Director · 2026-11-11 · 3 min read

In short

A road is not one material but a stack of layers. The natural ground (subgrade) is prepared first, then a sub-base and base of compacted aggregate spread the traffic load, and a surface layer protects the road from weather and wear. Drainage runs through all of it. The project specification sets the exact layers and thicknesses.

  • Each layer spreads load so the ground below is never overstressed.
  • Compaction and water control matter as much as the material itself.
  • The surface is only the top of the structure. Failures often start below it.

Start at the bottom: the subgrade

The subgrade is the natural ground, or filled earth, on which the road rests. It is shaped to the designed levels and compacted. A weak or wet subgrade is the most common root of road problems, so it is tested and, where needed, improved before anything is laid on it.

Sub-base and base: the load-spreading layers

Above the subgrade come layers of granular material, such as crushed stone aggregate. They are spread in layers, watered and rolled until firm. Their job is to spread the weight of vehicles over a wider area of ground.

The surface course

The top layer, either bituminous or concrete, gives a smooth riding surface and keeps water out of the layers below. It takes the direct wear of tyres and weather.

Drainage runs through everything

  • A camber or slope lets rain run off the surface
  • Side drains carry water away from the road edge
  • Culverts take water across or under the road
  • Water that stays in the layers weakens them

Why the order of work matters

Each layer is finished and checked before the next one is laid. Earthwork, aggregate laying, watering and rolling are the steps you see on a road site long before the final surface appears.

Common questions

What are the main layers of a road?

Typically the subgrade, a sub-base, a base, and a surface course. The design sets which layers a particular road needs.

Why is aggregate watered and rolled?

Water helps the particles pack tightly, and rolling compacts them, so the layer is firm and carries load without settling.

Why do roads fail?

Common causes are a weak subgrade, poor compaction, and water getting into the layers because drainage is inadequate.
