How to choose a civil contractor: questions to ask

Planning · By Mahendra Heda, Executive Director · 2026-11-11 · 3 min read

In short

Choosing a contractor on price alone is risky. Look at registration and eligibility, relevant experience, the team and equipment available, how the contractor plans time and cost, and how it handles quality. Ask to see work, not just claims, and speak to previous clients where you can.

  • Check registration and eligibility for the type and size of work.
  • Ask for relevant experience, and ask to visit completed or ongoing sites.
  • Look at how the contractor plans time, manpower and machinery.

Registration and eligibility

Government work requires the contractor to be registered in the right class. Private clients should also ask for relevant registrations, tax and statutory compliance.

Experience that matches your work

A road contractor is not automatically the right choice for a hospital. Ask for work of a similar type and scale, and where possible visit sites.

People and equipment

  • Does the contractor have its own team of engineers and supervisors?
  • Does it own or have access to the machinery your project needs?
  • Who will be on site day to day?

Planning

Ask how the contractor will schedule the work, and how it plans manpower and machinery to stay on schedule. A realistic plan matters more than a promise.

Quality

  • What tests and checks are done, and how are they recorded?
  • How are defects handled?
  • How does the contractor deal with changes during the project?

Communication

Good contractors answer questions clearly, report progress honestly and raise problems early.

Common questions

Should I choose the lowest price?

Price matters, but an unrealistically low price can lead to delays, disputes or poor quality. Compare scope and approach as well.

What registration do government contractors need?

Registration with the relevant department in the class suited to the work. Requirements vary, so check the tender conditions.

How can I verify experience?

Ask for completion records, visit sites, and speak to previous clients.
