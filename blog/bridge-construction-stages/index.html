Bridge construction stages explained

Roads · By Mahendra Heda, Executive Director · 2026-11-11 · 3 min read

In short

A bridge is built from the ground up in stages. Foundations carry loads to the ground, piers and abutments rise from them, and the deck is placed on top. Finishing works such as bearings, expansion joints, railings and the wearing surface complete the bridge. The design drawings define each stage.

  • Foundation work is first and most critical, because everything above depends on it.
  • The substructure (foundation, piers, abutments) carries the superstructure (deck).
  • Finishing details decide how well the bridge performs and ages.

Foundations

The foundation transfers the bridge's loads to the ground. Its type depends on the soil, the river or valley conditions and the loads. Work in or near water needs special planning for access and safety.

Piers and abutments

Piers are the supports between the ends of the bridge. Abutments are the supports at the ends, where the bridge meets the road. Both are built from reinforced concrete in stages, using formwork.

The deck

The deck is the part vehicles travel on. It rests on the piers and abutments through bearings. Depending on the design it may be cast in place or assembled from precast units.

Finishing works

  • Bearings that allow controlled movement
  • Expansion joints that let the deck expand and contract
  • Wearing surface, drainage spouts and railings
  • Approach roads and slope protection at the ends

Why sequence and quality matter

Hidden work, such as reinforcement and foundations, cannot be inspected once concrete covers it. Careful checking at each stage is how bridges are built to last.

Common questions

What are the main parts of a bridge?

The foundation, the piers and abutments (substructure), the deck (superstructure), and finishing items such as bearings, joints and railings.

What is an abutment?

The support at either end of a bridge, where the deck meets the approach road.

Why are bearings and expansion joints needed?

They let the deck move slightly with temperature and loads without damaging the structure.
