Storm drain planning in layouts: what to get right

Water · By Mahendra Heda, Executive Director · 2026-11-11 · 3 min read

In short

A storm drain carries rainwater away from plots and roads. In a layout, it should be planned together with road levels, plot levels and the point where water leaves the site. Done late, drainage causes waterlogging and expensive rework. Done early, it is a small part of the civil work.

  • Storm drains should follow the ground slope, and be planned with road and plot levels.
  • Every drain needs a safe outfall where the water can leave the layout.
  • Drains need access for cleaning, and sized for the heaviest rain, not the average.

What a storm drain does

When rain falls on roads, plots and paved areas, it runs off instead of soaking in. A storm drain collects that runoff and carries it to a place where it can leave the layout without harm.

Start from levels

Water flows downhill. The first step is a survey of ground levels, so road levels, plot levels and drain levels all agree on where water will go. A drain that is laid without checking levels can hold water instead of moving it.

Points to settle during planning

  • The outfall: where the water leaves the layout, and whether that point can take it
  • The route of the main drain along the road network
  • Drain size, based on the area that drains into it and the heaviest expected rain
  • Inlets that let water from the road reach the drain
  • Access points for cleaning and inspection

Common problems

  • Drains laid at the wrong slope
  • No outfall, or an outfall blocked by later construction
  • Drains too small for heavy rain
  • Silt and waste with no access to clear them

Build it with the roads

Drain construction and road construction share earthwork and levels. Building them together avoids cutting finished roads later and keeps the layout's civil work coordinated. Storm drain works in layouts are part of what MCC Group undertakes.

Common questions

What is a storm drain?

A storm drain is a channel or pipe that carries rainwater away from roads, plots and paved areas.

Why should drains be planned before roads?

Road levels and drain levels depend on each other. Planning them together avoids rework.

What is an outfall?

The outfall is the point where a drain discharges water out of the layout, for example into a larger drain or a natural channel.

Do drains need maintenance?

Yes. Silt and waste collect over time, so drains need access points for cleaning.
