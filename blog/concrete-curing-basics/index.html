Concrete curing basics: why the first days matter

Quality · By Mahendra Heda, Executive Director · 2026-11-11 · 3 min read

In short

Curing means keeping concrete moist and at a suitable temperature after it is placed, so the cement keeps reacting and the concrete gains strength and durability. Concrete that dries out too early stays weaker and more prone to cracking. The curing method and duration are set by the specification.

  • Concrete hardens by a reaction with water, not by drying.
  • Early drying causes surface cracks and weak surfaces.
  • Curing is cheap compared with the cost of repairing poor concrete.

Concrete does not dry, it cures

Cement reacts with water to form the material that binds the concrete. The reaction needs water to continue. If the surface dries out, the reaction stops near the surface, and the concrete there is weaker.

Common curing methods

  • Keeping the surface continuously wet by sprinkling or ponding
  • Covering with wet hessian or sand
  • Plastic sheeting that holds moisture in
  • Curing compounds sprayed on the surface

Weather matters

Hot, dry and windy weather dries concrete fast. Cool weather slows the reaction. Both need attention. Shade, wind shelter and extra watering help in hot weather.

Where it matters most

Large exposed surfaces such as concrete paving and canal lining lose moisture quickly, so curing is critical. Joints in lining and paving must also be formed properly and protected.

A simple rule

Plan curing before the concrete is placed: who does it, with what water, and for how long. It should not be an afterthought.

Common questions

What is curing?

Keeping fresh concrete moist and at a suitable temperature so it keeps gaining strength.

How long should concrete be cured?

It depends on the cement, the mix and the weather. The project specification gives the duration.

What happens if concrete is not cured?

It can be weaker, less durable, and may crack on the surface.
