Equipment planning on infrastructure sites

Planning · By Mahendra Heda, Executive Director · 2026-11-11 · 3 min read

In short

Machines are a large part of the cost of infrastructure work. Good equipment planning matches the machines to the work in each stage, balances loaders with haulers so none wait, schedules maintenance, and avoids hiring or buying more than the work needs. The goal is steady output at controlled cost.

  • Match machines to the work in each stage, not to the peak of the whole project.
  • Balance machines that depend on each other, such as excavators and tippers.
  • Maintenance planned in advance prevents breakdowns that stop the whole crew.

Different work, different machines

  • Excavators for digging and loading
  • Tipper trucks for hauling
  • Road rollers for compaction
  • Water tankers for dust control and moisture in layers
  • Batching plants and mixers for concrete

Balance the chain

Machines work in chains. An excavator loads tippers, which haul to a roller. If one link is too slow, the others wait. The aim is to balance the chain, not to maximise any single machine.

Plan by stage

Earthwork needs many excavators and trucks. Later stages may need rollers and concrete equipment. Planning by stage means each machine is on site when needed and released or moved when not.

Maintenance and spares

A machine that breaks down at a critical moment holds up everyone who depends on it. Regular servicing and key spares on site are cheaper than idle crews.

Own or hire

Owning machines gives control and availability. Hiring gives flexibility for peaks. The right mix depends on the volume and continuity of work.

Common questions

Why plan machinery by stage?

Different stages need different machines. Planning by stage avoids idle equipment and shortages.

What is a machine chain?

A sequence of dependent machines, such as an excavator, tippers and a roller. Output depends on keeping the chain balanced.

Is owning machinery better than hiring?

It depends on how continuously the work needs the machine. Many contractors use a mix.
